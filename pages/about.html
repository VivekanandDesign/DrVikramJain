FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about rheumatology and immunology

Who is a rheumatologist?

A rheumatologist is a specialist physician who deals with diagnosis and treatment of arthritis and other diseases affecting the immune system, together called as autoimmune rheumatic diseases.

What causes autoimmune rheumatic diseases?

Your body has an immune system that protects you from disease and infection. However, due to various reasons, the immune system can attack the healthy cells of the body, causing what we call autoimmune diseases.

Which parts of the body are affected?

Arthritis mainly affects the joints while other musculoskeletal conditions can affect the muscles, tendons, and ligaments. These diseases can also affect other parts of the body, including eyes, skin, internal organs, and the nervous system.

What are the common diseases treated by rheumatologists?

Rheumatologists see over 100 different types of autoimmune diseases affecting both adults as well as children, of which the following are the most common:

  • Inflammatory arthritis – Rheumatoid arthritis, Gout, Psoriatic arthritis , Reactive arthritis, Ankylosing spondylitis
  • Systemic autoimmune diseases – Lupus , Scleroderma, Vasculitis, Sjogren’s syndrome , Myositis
  • Generalized pain conditions – Fibromyalgia and regional pain conditions affecting low back, neck ,shoulder, hip & foot
  • Degenerative joint disorders – Osteoarthritis
  • Other diseases affecting immune system – Allergic disorders and Primary immunodeficiency diseases
